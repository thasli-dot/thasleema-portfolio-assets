The Instinct Behind the Title

STAGE 1
Ran discovery before I knew the term
As an educator, every session was a live experiment: which schedule kept students showing up, which teaching moment moved retention, which nudge pushed someone deeper into the course instead of dropping off. I was reading behavior and shipping fixes — I just did not call it a funnel yet.
STAGE 2
Treated feedback like a sprint cadence
Materials, pacing, delivery — all rebuilt on every cycle of real feedback, not once a year. Retention and revenue moved because the loop never stopped. Same discipline as agile iteration, minus the ceremony and the title.
STAGE 3
Built a zero-CAC business on positioning, not budget
Took that instinct and built a venture: freemium model, zero paid acquisition, 90% retention, 100% referral-driven growth. Competitive analysis found a low-cost, high-volume position competitors were not occupying — engineered around real "wow" moments, not discounts.
STAGE 4
Used GenAI as a growth lever, before it was a job requirement
Learned prompting early to generate visual concept notes, crisp explainer videos, and quick-reference cheat sheets — content that measurably drove deeper adoption. Not a resume line. A distribution channel.
STAGE 5
Formalized the instinct — did not discover it
An AI PM course did not teach me product thinking. It gave structure, vocabulary, and technical depth to five years of decisions I was already making — discovery, iteration, GTM, retention — under different job titles.

About Me

Who I am?

I spent three years as an educator for UPSC aspirants across India — and somewhere in that work, I became a product person without knowing it. Every day was an exercise in taking a complex, high-stakes system and turning it into a simple, actionable decision for someone with very little margin for error.

Then I built something: a boutique EdTech business, from zero, that hit 90% retention and grew entirely through word of mouth — no ad spend, no growth hacks, just a product people did not want to leave. That is when I realized I had been doing product management for years without the title.

Now I am formalizing that instinct — five live case studies, working prototypes, and PRDs built using industry-standard product management practices — because the highest-leverage thing I can do next is build infrastructure for the users I have spent years understanding.
